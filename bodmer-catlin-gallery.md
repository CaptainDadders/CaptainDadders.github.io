## How to Use This Catalog

Both artists traveled the Missouri River after the expedition and painted the same landscapes, landmarks, and tribes the Corps encountered — but not at the same time. The temporal gap is the key caveat for each entry.

**To find a candidate:** look up the expedition date range, find entries whose subject matter overlaps (same river location, same tribe, same type of scene), and use the listed source page to retrieve and verify the image.

**To get full-resolution images:** lewis-clark.org thumbnails are low-res. For publication-quality versions, use:
- **Smithsonian Open Access** — `americanart.si.edu` or `ids.si.edu` (free, high-res, confirmed public domain)
- **Wikimedia Commons** — search artist name + painting title
- **Joslyn Art Museum** — primary holder of Bodmer originals; some available via their digital collections

## Karl Bodmer (1809–1893)

**Who:** Swiss artist hired by Prince Maximilian von Wied to document the upper Missouri.  
**When he traveled:** 1832–34 — approximately **28–30 years after the expedition**.  
**What he painted:** landscapes, river scenes, Native American portraits, encampments, ceremonies — all at the same locations the Corps visited.  
**Public domain status:** Yes. Bodmer died 1893; all works well past copyright. Engravings published in Maximilian's 1839–41 *Travels* are also public domain.  
**Key caveat:** The landscape is essentially the same; the tribes (Mandan, Hidatsa, Arikara, Assiniboine, Lakota) are the same nations, though their circumstances had changed in three decades. Caption must note "painted 1832–34, ~30 years after the expedition."  
**Attribution:** "Karl Bodmer, *[title]*, 1832–34. Public domain. [Holding institution]."

## George Catlin (1796–1872)

**Who:** American painter who set out to document Native American life before it disappeared.  
**When he traveled:** 1830–32 up the Missouri — approximately **26–28 years after the expedition**.  
**What he painted:** tribal portraits, ceremonies, buffalo hunts, landscapes, village scenes — same nations and many of the same locations.  
**Public domain status:** Yes. Catlin died 1872; all works public domain. Primary collections at Smithsonian American Art Museum (SAAM) and Smithsonian National Portrait Gallery — all available via Smithsonian Open Access.  
**Key caveat:** Same as Bodmer — same places and tribes, not the same moment. Catlin's Mandan subjects include individuals (Black Moccasin, Four Bears/Mato-Tope) who were adults during the expedition. Caption must note "painted c. 1830–32, ~28 years after the expedition."  
**Attribution:** "George Catlin, *[title]*, c. [year]. Public domain. Smithsonian American Art Museum." (or other holding institution as appropriate)
